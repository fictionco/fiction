# Copy & Writing Rules

## Positioning & Intent

- Before writing copy, create a plan that considers who the product is for, what they know, what they're looking for, who they want to become, and what they want to achieve.
- Assert premium positioning. Put the offer up front. We are selling a premium product, not begging for customers.
- Imply what it does, who it's for, and why it matters using heuristics, metaphors, outcomes, and sharp comparisons.
- Scientific Advertising: Think like a direct salesperson. Speak to a half-sold user — no cleverness, no decoration, no fluff. Just persuasion.

## Language & Style

- Prefer **concrete** over clever. Be clear, not poetic. Avoid clichés.
- For images, videos, and figures, they should Demo capabilities. You can show them small and have them popup.
- Use **visual and emotional language** sparingly but strategically. Words like: see, feel, imagine, story, reveal.
- Allow users to **conclude the value themselves** through vivid, real descriptions.
- Avoid saying "marketing," "branding," or other vague, low-trust terms unless clarified.
- Use NLP evocative, visual language and power words that are **non-cliché**, emotionally resonant, and action-oriented

## Copy Heuristics

- Copy should read at a **12-year-old level**.
- Use **active voice**, with beautiful flow.
- Avoid vague terms: "marketing," "branding," "platform," "solution," "empower."
- Use words that describe **outcomes**, not categories.

## Headlines

### Primary Headlines (H1)
The main headline MUST be specific about what the product does and the concrete outcome. It should:
- State the exact deliverables (bio, site, newsletter)
- Include specific metrics (3 minutes, $79/month, 4x results)
- Make the value proposition immediately clear
- Example: "Fiction builds your bio, site, newsletter, and authority — in 3 minutes."

### Supporting Headlines (H2-H3)
Secondary headlines can use emotional hooks and pain points to create resonance:

#### 1. Start with the unspoken pain
- Surface the hidden cost or internal friction the user hasn't named yet.
- Example: Your LinkedIn gets 200 views. Zero replies, zero leads, zero point.

#### 2. Draw a clear line in the sand
- Frame yourself against bloated, broken, or outdated tools to create contrast.
- Example: Not another personal site. A system that earns you recognition.

#### 3. Lead with the hard result
- Quantify the gain — time saved, output produced, attention earned.
- Example: 3 investor intros, 2 advisory requests, 5 subscribers — first week.

#### 4. Say what they're already thinking
- Mirror a stuck thought, doubt, or frustration they've privately repeated.
- Example: You know you should be getting more inbound. You just don't.

#### 5. Frame the transformation (before → after)
- Describe the shift in identity, visibility, or perceived value.
- Example: From ignored expert to booked solid — automatically.

#### 6. Speak to past failures and future control
- Acknowledge what's never worked, then show what flips that script.
- Example: Stop hoping someone Googles you. Start showing up on your terms.

#### 7. Make it feel rare, premium, and not for everyone
- Use price, tone, or framing to signal exclusivity and confidence.
- Example: $79/month. Because your reputation is worth more than free tools.

#### 8. Show specific, real-world outcomes
- Avoid abstraction — anchor in actual use cases and visible results.
- Example: Weekly: 12 profile views → 47. Monthly: 0 inbound → 8 qualified leads.

#### 9. Call out high-leverage personas
- Lead with the most time-poor, status-conscious, or ROI-driven users.
- Example: Built for advisors, operators, and dealmakers who don't self-promote.

#### 10. Make the outcome self-evident
- Write headlines that show, not tell — the payoff should feel obvious in seconds.
- Example: Your expertise, packaged. Your content, automated. Your inbound, solved.

### Headline Hierarchy
1. **Primary (H1)**: Always specific product description + concrete outcome
2. **Secondary (H2)**: Emotional hooks, transformations, pain points
3. **Tertiary (H3)**: Feature benefits, social proof, specific results
