# Research Tools

## When to Use Research

Use research tools to improve outputs when:
- **Validating claims** about market trends or user behavior
- **Finding competitor positioning** and messaging patterns
- **Getting current data** for copy that needs to feel fresh
- **Backing up contrarian takes** with actual evidence
- **Understanding search intent** for landing pages
- **Finding concrete examples** instead of generic ones

IMPORTANT: AVOID false positives, prefer actual data. Don't trust media, social media claims, or anything that is likely promotional or herd thinking.

## Available Tools

### Perplexity Research
- **Use for**: Market trends, competitor analysis, industry insights
- **When**: Copy needs current data or contrarian positioning needs backing
- **Example**: "What trends are seeing actual adoption and growth? Why?"

### DataForSEO
- **Use for**: Keyword research, SERP analysis, competitor SEO data
- **When**: Building landing pages that need to rank or convert
- **Example**: Search volume for "personal branding" vs "professional presence"

### Web Scraping/Fetch
- **Use for**: Analyzing competitor copy, pricing, positioning
- **When**: Need specific examples of what works/doesn't work
- **Example**: How do top personal brand tools position themselves?

## Research Principles

- **Research to support, not replace** your contrarian instincts
- **Look for concrete examples** over abstract trends
- **Find gaps others missed** — what are competitors not saying?
- **Use data to sharpen positioning** — not to follow the crowd
- **Validate user pain points** with real search queries and forum posts

## Quick Research Workflow

1. **Define the claim** you want to validate
2. **Search for contrarian evidence** first
3. **Look for concrete examples** and specific data
4. **Find what competitors avoid** saying
5. **Use findings to sharpen** your positioning, not soften it

*Research should make you more confident in bold moves, not more conservative.*
